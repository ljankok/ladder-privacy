Nourish — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Nourish is an iPhone nutrition and wellbeing app developed by Lucio Jankok. You do not need an account to use it.

Your diary and profile

Your onboarding answers, including measurements, goals, allergies or intolerances and health-screening answers, are saved on your iPhone. Your food diary, water records, weights and check-ins are also stored locally. Nourish does not upload these records to a developer-operated server or enable app-managed iCloud diary sync.

These private app files use iOS file protection and are excluded from iCloud backup. App preferences and cached public product information are stored separately on the device and may be included in normal device backups. The app does not add advertising, analytics or tracking SDKs, and does not sell your personal information.

Apple Health is optional

If you connect Apple Health, Nourish requests access to the specific measurements you choose, such as weight, height, steps and active energy. Imported readings are held in memory; measurements you explicitly use for your profile become part of the locally stored profile.

Sharing your logged nutrition and water with Health is a separate choice. If enabled and permitted, Nourish writes logged dietary energy, protein, carbohydrates, fat and water, and updates or removes its own samples when the corresponding diary records change. It does not write calorie targets as intake. Local export receipts help reconcile the app’s own Health samples.

You can turn sharing off in Nourish and change or revoke permissions in Apple Health. Turning sharing off, disconnecting Health or deleting Nourish does not erase samples already saved in Health. Manage those records in Health. Apple manages Health storage and any Health synchronization under its own settings and privacy terms.

Barcode lookup and the camera

Camera images are processed on your iPhone to recognize a barcode. Nourish does not upload camera images. You can choose barcode digits with scroll controls instead of using the camera.

For a product that is not already cached, the app sends the barcode number to Open Food Facts over HTTPS to retrieve public nutrition information. Like other internet requests, this exposes your IP address and request headers to that service. Your profile, diary, health-screening answers and Apple Health readings are not included in the lookup request. Results are cached locally.

Open Food Facts operates independently and its own privacy terms apply to its service. Opening product pages, reference sources or other external links also connects you to the chosen website, which receives normal web-request information. A barcode result is not an allergy-safety verification.

Water reminders

If you choose reminders and allow notifications, Nourish schedules local notifications on the device. It does not send your hydration routine to a developer notification server. You can disable reminders in the app or notifications in iOS Settings.

TestFlight feedback

During beta testing, Apple’s TestFlight service may provide the developer with testing metrics, crash reports and feedback that you submit, including screenshots or contact details where applicable. This is separate from the app’s local diary storage and is governed by Apple’s TestFlight terms and privacy information. Feedback sent by email is received at the address you contact and used to respond to the request or investigate the issue.

Your choices and deletion

You can remove food and water entries in the app and edit your profile. To remove Nourish’s remaining local app data, delete the app from the iPhone; offloading the app preserves its data. Records already written to Apple Health and any TestFlight feedback or emails are separate and are not erased by deleting the app. Contact the developer about feedback or correspondence you have sent.

Nourish’s automated planning is intended for supported adult profiles. It does not knowingly collect children’s information on a developer-operated server. Where automatic planning is unavailable, the app directs users to individual guidance.
